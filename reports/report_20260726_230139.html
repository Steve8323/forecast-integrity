What this measures. A forecasting system cannot be evaluated on accuracy until its questions resolve. This audit scores one anyway, using three things that are checkable on day zero: whether its probabilities obey the probability axioms, whether an arbitrage can be constructed against them, and whether its apparent skill is recall of events it already knows about.

The forecaster is asked each question in a separate process with no memory of the others, so it cannot paper over an inconsistency by looking at what it just said. Its answers are then sealed in a Merkle commitment before any analysis touches them.

Limitations

  • Coherence is necessary, not sufficient. A forecaster that answers 0.5 everywhere is perfectly coherent and perfectly useless. This audit is a filter, not a ranking.
  • The contamination gap is an upper bound on leakage, not a point estimate: the resolved controls are famous events while the open questions are deliberately near-even, so some of the gap is the past genuinely being more knowable. The clean version is the matched-difficulty pre/post-cutoff split, which becomes runnable when this set resolves.
  • The date probe partly measures where the model's knowledge cutoff sits, not only leakage.
  • Accuracy, calibration and horizon decay are unmeasured by construction. That is the point of the commitment: they become measurable later, on forecasts that were sealed first.
